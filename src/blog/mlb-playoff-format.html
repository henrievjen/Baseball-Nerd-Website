How the MLB Playoffs Work: Wild Cards, Division Series & the World Series Explained

By Henri Evjen  ·  Baseball Nerd  ·  7 min read

Major League Baseball expanded its playoff format in 2022 from 10 teams to 12 teams, adding a new Wild Card round that changed how the postseason works — and how regular-season records translate into playoff success. If you haven't followed baseball closely since before 2022, or if you're new to the sport entirely, the current format may seem confusing at first. This guide walks through every round from start to World Series champion.

How Teams Qualify: The 12-Team Field

Twelve teams — six from the American League and six from the National League — qualify for the MLB postseason each year. The six spots in each league are filled as follows:

  • 3 division winners — The team with the best record in each of the three divisions (East, Central, West) earns a division title and a playoff spot. Division winners are seeded 1 through 3 based on their overall record.
  • 3 wild card teams — The three teams in each league with the best records that did NOT win a division earn wild card spots. They are seeded 4 through 6.

The division winners with the two best records in each league (seeds 1 and 2) receive a critical advantage: they earn a first-round bye and skip directly to the Division Series. The #3 seed (division winner with the worst record) and all three wild card teams (seeds 4–6) must play in the Wild Card Series.

Round 1: The Wild Card Series (Best of 3)

The Wild Card Series is a best-of-3 series played entirely at the home park of the higher-seeded team. The matchups are:

  • #3 seed vs #6 seed — all three games at the #3 seed's park
  • #4 seed vs #5 seed — all three games at the #4 seed's park

There is no travel in the Wild Card Series. If the series goes three games, all three are played at the same stadium. This heavily favors the higher-seeded team, who never has to play a road game and who benefits from home crowd support in every game. Winning the Wild Card round means winning just two of the three games.

The Wild Card Series is one of the most high-pressure rounds in baseball because of its brevity. A great starting pitcher can dominate one or even two starts in a three-game series, and a bad start by the other team's ace can effectively end a series before momentum can shift. There are no off-days in the Wild Card Series — games can be played on consecutive days, which limits bullpen management and recovery.

Round 2: The Division Series (Best of 5)

The two teams that received first-round byes (#1 and #2 seeds) now enter the bracket alongside the two Wild Card Series winners. The Division Series (ALDS in the AL, NLDS in the NL) is a best-of-5 series with home games going to the higher-seeded team.

The format is 2-2-1: two games at the higher seed's park, two at the lower seed's park, and if needed, a fifth and deciding game back at the higher seed's park. There is one off-day in the series between games 2 and 3 (for travel) and sometimes another between games 4 and 5.

Winning two division titles in the same league gives teams a potentially significant rest advantage. The teams with byes have been resting and preparing for up to a week while the wild card teams have been grinding through three consecutive days of games. In theory this favors the top seeds; in practice, the extra rest can cut both ways, sometimes leaving top seeds rusty after extended time off.

Round 3: The League Championship Series (Best of 7)

The two Division Series winners in each league meet for the League Championship Series — the ALCS and NLCS. The winner of each LCS earns a pennant (one of the oldest trophies in American sports, dating to the 1870s) and advances to the World Series. The LCS is a best-of-7, using a 2-3-2 format: two games at the higher seed, three at the lower seed, then up to two back at the higher seed.

The LCS is where rotations begin to matter in a more significant way. With seven potential games over up to nine days (including travel days), managers must decide how to deploy their starting pitchers: do you use your ace three times or try to hold him for a potential Game 7? How aggressively do you use your bullpen in a Game 1 blowout when you might need those arms in a close Game 7? These strategic decisions are what make the LCS the most tactically complex round in the postseason.

The World Series (Best of 7)

The American League champion and National League champion meet for the World Series — the oldest championship in American professional sports, played continuously since 1903 (with gaps only in 1904 and 1919–1920). The format is identical to the LCS: best of 7, 2-3-2 home game structure.

Home field advantage in the World Series goes to the team with the better regular-season record, or to the team from the league designated to host in alternating years if records are equal. Home field advantage means playing Games 1, 2, 6, and 7 at home — all the potential clinching games are at the home park.

Historically, home field advantage in the World Series provides a measurable but not decisive edge. Teams with home field win the World Series at roughly a 55–60% rate — meaningful, but far from guaranteed. The quality of the teams matters far more than the location of the games.

Tracking the Standings Race

Because of the expanded playoff format, far more teams stay in genuine playoff contention through September than under the old system. A team that is 6–7 games out of a division lead in early September might still be very much alive in the wild card race — and there are now three wild card spots to chase.

Key Strategic Implications of the Current Format

The 12-team format has changed how general managers and managers think about the regular season:

  • The bye is enormously valuable. Earning a top-2 seed means skipping a brutal best-of-3 where anything can happen and resting your pitchers. Teams now target the top-2 seed as aggressively as a division title.
  • The #3 seed is arguably the worst position. A division winner who earned the 3 seed must play a Wild Card Series against a hot wild card team with no home-field advantage benefit (all three games are still at home, but the lower-seeded wild card team is often playing with nothing to lose).
  • Pitching depth matters more than ever. In the Wild Card Series (best of 3, no off-days), you may need to burn your best relievers heavily. Teams with strong bullpens are disproportionately successful in the expanded format.
  • Regular-season stats are less predictive. With 12 teams and multiple rounds, a hot team that peaks in October can beat a better team in a short series more often than in a longer format. The champion is not always the most talented team — they're the one that gets the right matchups and stays healthy at the right time.
