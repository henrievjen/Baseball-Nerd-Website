Background

I'm a lifelong baseball fan, longtime card collector, and software developer based in the United States. I've followed the sport closely for as long as I can remember — collecting cards since I was a kid, tracking box scores long before I could code, and eventually building the app I always wished existed: a fast, clean way to check scores and stats without wading through pop-ups, auto-play video, and clutter.

Baseball Nerd started as a personal project to solve that one problem and grew from there. I design, build, and maintain every part of it myself — the live score cards, the standings and stats pipelines, the stadium weather maps, the Android app, and the guides published under Guides.

Why I Write About the Numbers

I'm not a professional statistician, and the guides on this site don't pretend otherwise — they're written by someone who has spent years in the box scores and, just as importantly, spent months wiring up the actual data pipeline that powers this site's stats, standings, and leaderboards. Building that pipeline means going stat by stat through what the MLB Stats API actually reports, which numbers agree with each other, and which ones (like OPS, FIP, or WAR) require combining several raw figures in a specific way to mean anything. That hands-on familiarity with where the numbers come from is what the articles on this site try to pass along in plain English.

Every guide is written to be useful to a reader who just wants to understand what a stat means and why it matters — not to show off jargon. Where a topic has genuine debate (like fWAR vs. bWAR, or how much a small sample should be trusted), the guides say so rather than presenting one number as gospel.
